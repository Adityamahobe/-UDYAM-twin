INTELLIGENT SMART FACTORY

Predictive AI, Digital Twins & Reinforcement Learning.

Bridging physics-based twins, explainable predictive analytics, and future autonomous decision support for brownfield Indian machine shops.

● Implemented 01

3D Digital Twin

A real-time spatial and mathematical replica of the 4-machine line. It mirrors active mechanical kinematics in Three.js WebGL while running nominal physics equations in parallel to predict what the machine should do under given rotational speeds and cutting loads.

✓ Dual Views: Operator guidance & Engineer telemetry
✓ Synchronisation: 7 concurrent Blender kinematic loops
● Implemented 02

Predictive Maintenance

Rather than raw threshold alarms, the twin evaluates residual drift (e = y - ŷ). Sustained thermal drift triggers bounded recalibration of cooling coefficients (h_cool), treating parameter drift itself as a diagnostic health signal before tool breakage or dimensional drift occurs.

✓ Random Forest (AI4I 2020 test set): loading metrics…
✓ Forecast Engine: Minutes to thermal/wear limit
● Implemented (simulation) 03

Reinforcement Learning

A proposed autonomous decision-support agent formulated as a Markov Decision Process (MDP). The agent safely explores feed-rate and spindle derate trade-offs in simulation, balancing part production quotas against cutting tool degradation and thermal strain.

✓ Reward Function: Throughput vs Tool Wear & Thermal Penalty
✓ Safety Gate: Advisory-only proposals in live mode
→ Staged Roadmap 04

Progressive Roadmap

A structured path from software demonstrator to shop-floor validation: Stage A (current software demonstrator), Stage B (NIT Raipur Mechanical workshop motor test), Stage C (Urla/Bhilai MSME read-only pilot), and Stage D (supervised closed-loop actuation).

    SIMPLE ARCHITECTURE

    A Better Way to Watch Your Factory.

    How sensor data becomes actionable manufacturing knowledge in three transparent steps.

    01

    See your factory

    Clamp-on sensors read motor current, vibration, and temperature non-invasively. Real-time readings stream into an interactive 3D model that reflects your actual shop floor.

    02

    Understand machine status

    The software compares incoming readings with calibrated physics models. It detects tool wear and cooling degradation early—before parts are cut out of tolerance.

    03

    Decide what needs attention

    Instead of confusing graphs, you receive plain-English guidance. You know whether to stop a machine now, check it at shift change, or continue running safely.

    PURPOSE & MISSION

    Designed for Independent Machine Shops.

    Small and medium factories form the backbone of industrial manufacturing. UDYAM-TWIN gives family-owned machine shops the same digital-twin intelligence used by aerospace primes—clean, affordable, and easy to use.

    UDYAM-TWIN is a browser-based digital twin platform designed specifically for MSME machine shops. It visualizes an interconnected 4-machine CNC production line in photorealistic 3D, continuously compares machine telemetry with physics models, and alerts owners in plain English before tools break or parts drift out of tolerance.
    Yes. The interactive 3D digital twin showcases a unified factory floor environment featuring all four machines: the Bar Saw (SAW-01), the CNC Turning Lathe (LATHE-01), the Vertical Machining Center (VMC-01), and the Shared Screw Air Compressor (COMP-01). You can view the entire floor overview or switch camera focus directly to any individual machine.
    In Testing mode, every reading comes live from the UDYAM-TWIN simulation (a physics model of the four machines, with the AI4I 2020 failure model). In Real Data mode the twin shows the Wokwi virtual devices; no real machine is connected yet.
    Not yet. Today it runs on its simulation and on Wokwi virtual ESP32 devices over MQTT. The path to real machines (clamp-on current and vibration sensors on an ESP32 or a PLC, over MQTT) is built but not yet used with hardware; it never controls a machine on its own.
    Every alert in UDYAM-TWIN is written in plain English, avoiding confusing error codes. Each advisory answers three practical questions: What happened (e.g. blade wear reached 42%), Why it matters (cutting remains accurate, but teeth will need changing in 80 cuts), and What you should do next (inspect during the next scheduled shift change).
